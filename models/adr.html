Advection-Diffusion-Reaction with Unfitted Interfaces

Description

The ADR model (proteus.ADR) solves linear advection-diffusion-reaction equations

\[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}u - a\nabla u) = f\]

on a background mesh. Besides the standard formulation, it can treat two kinds of interfaces that the mesh does not follow. Both are described by signed distance functions, so moving an interface needs no new mesh.

  • Embedded boundary \(\Gamma_s\) (CutFEM). The part of the domain where the signed distance is negative (a solid) is removed. A Dirichlet value is imposed weakly on \(\Gamma_s\) by Nitsche’s method, or a Robin condition is applied instead. A ghost penalty on the interior edges of the active mesh that belong to a cut cell keeps cells with tiny fluid parts stable.

  • Immersed interface \(\Gamma_f\) (immersed finite elements, IFEM). The diffusion coefficient jumps from \(\mu_a\) to \(\mu_b\) across \(\Gamma_f\). Cut cells use a two-branch IFEM basis, for \(P_1\) and \(P_2\), that satisfies the interface conditions. The symmetric and consistent edge terms of SCIFEM restore consistency on edges cut by \(\Gamma_f\), and an interior penalty stabilizes them for \(P_2\). Prescribed jumps of the solution and of the flux are supported.

With both interfaces, as for a floating body at the water surface, the domain holds two fluid phases and a solid, and a cell can be cut by both curves (a triply-cut cell). Such cells are integrated with composite equivalent polynomials and receive a consistency correction on the edges cut by \(\Gamma_f\). In the paper’s tests this hybrid CutFEM-IFEM method converges at the optimal orders for \(p=1,2\) and coefficient contrasts up to 1000: \(p+1\) in \(L^2\) and \(p\) in the broken \(H^1\) seminorm. The \(L^\infty\) error also decreases at order \(p+1\) on average, with fluctuating rates at the highest contrast.

All options are arguments of proteus.ADR.Coefficients; its documentation lists them with the values used in the paper below.

Running the test problems

The drivers are in test/ci; the first two reproduce every table of the paper:

  • ladr_3p_2d_p.py with ladr_3p_2d_c0pk_n.py: the unfitted method. The option paper=True sets the paper’s penalty parameters and edge-term choices.

  • ladr_fitted_2d_p.py with ladr_fitted_2d_n.py: the same problems on a mesh fitted to both interfaces, for comparison.

  • ladr_ss_2d_p.py with ladr_ss_2d_c0p1_n.py or ladr_ss_2d_c0p2_n.py: two-phase problems with an immersed interface only.

For example, Test 1 of the paper with quadratic elements on a structured mesh:

% cd test/ci
% parun ladr_3p_2d_p.py ladr_3p_2d_c0pk_n.py -C "paper=True test=2.0 pOrder=2 nCells=40"

The test case is selected with test (2.0: Test 1; 2.1 with test21solutionJump and test21fluxJump: Tests 2 and 3; 3.0: Test 4, Robin condition), the coefficient contrast with mub, the degree with pOrder, and the mesh with nCells, refinement and unstructured. All options and their defaults are listed, with a short description, at the top of ladr_3p_2d_p.py. With an analytical solution the model logs the \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) errors, and the broken \(H^1\) seminorm when the exact gradient is available.

In parallel, the ghost penalty and the SCIFEM edge terms need two layers of mesh overlap; the drivers set this, and the model stops with an error if a mesh has fewer.

The tests in test/IFEM (test_ifem.py for two phases, test_ifem_3p.py for three phases, including two-rank runs) check these cases against stored values, and test/test_equivalent_polynomials.py checks the cut-cell integration.

Reference

  • D. Nathawani and C. E. Kees, Hybrid CutFEM-IFEM model for embedded and immersed interfaces with triply-cut cells for the 2D Poisson problem (2026), submitted.
